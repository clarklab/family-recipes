# How to write recipe Notes

These cards were written by grandparents and great-grandparents, many with little schooling.
The family will be happy to have whatever is on the card. The Notes section should read like a
warm, useful margin, not a proofreading report. Never comment on spelling, grammar, neatness, or
what the writer left out.

## Keep (short, plain, kind)

- Who it's from or who signed it, dates, "From the kitchen of …", "Mom says …", "Enjoy ♥".
  If a person is named as the source and the front matter has no `from:` line, you may add one
  right after `card:` (for example `from: Della Mitchell`). Don't add phone numbers, addresses or
  other contact details anywhere.
- Real cooking information that is on the card but not already in Ingredients/Directions: yields,
  servings, pan sizes, tips ("A wire cake cooler is good"), storage, "serve warm", occasions.
- Handwritten additions that change or extend the recipe, stated simply as useful information:
  "Amounts for a triple batch are written in the margin: 3 cups brown sugar, 3 eggs, …"
  or "Also written on the card: or 2 oz. Gruyère."
- Where a printed recipe came from, in a few words: "From a Dr Pepper recipe card (1966)."
- Another version of the same recipe on a different card: "Another version is on card 0075."
  (Keep these; turn any "Possible duplicate: … Keep both, merge them, or drop one" line into this form.)
- Something genuinely charming about the card, at most one line: "Written on the back of a
  Hallmark photo-album page."
- Explanations of old abbreviations or terms that help a modern cook: "wh. means white",
  "oleo is margarine", "a No. 303 can is about 2 cups".

## Drop

- Spelling, typo, grammar fixes ("Spelling fixed: …"), and any explanation of a word choice.
- Correction tape, overwritten or crossed-out digits, rubbed or faded words, words running off the
  edge, cut-off scans, which side was scanned ("only the front was scanned"), upside-down or
  sideways text, rotation.
- Anything about what's missing or incomplete: no amounts, no method, no baking time, servings
  not given, "the card doesn't say". (Exception: if the card itself says something like
  "The rest of the recipe was not there", you may quote it.)
- Why an uncertain reading is uncertain. The `[…?]` marks in the text already flag those for
  review; leave every `[…?]` mark in Ingredients/Directions/title exactly as it is.
- Ink colors, typewritten vs handwritten, paper type, stains, unless it's the one charming line above.
- Transcriber explanations ("I titled it…", "I assumed…", "the step grouping follows…").
- Descriptions of unrelated things on the other side of the card.

## Form

- Usually 0–3 bullets. If nothing is left, delete the whole `## Notes` section (heading included).
- Write plainly and kindly. No "appears to", "possibly", "it seems" unless truly needed.
- Don't touch anything outside the `## Notes` section except adding a `from:` line as described above.
